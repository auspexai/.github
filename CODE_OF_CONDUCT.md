# Contributor Covenant Code of Conduct

## Our Pledge

We as members, contributors, and leaders pledge to make participation in our community a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, color, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming, diverse, inclusive, and healthy community.

## Our Standards

Examples of behavior that contributes to a positive environment for our community include:

* Demonstrating empathy and kindness toward other people
* Being respectful of differing opinions, viewpoints, and experiences
* Giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback
* Accepting responsibility and apologizing to those affected by our mistakes, and learning from the experience
* Focusing on what is best not just for us as individuals, but for the overall community

Examples of unacceptable behavior include:

* The use of sexualized language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of any kind
* Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks
* Public or private harassment
* Publishing others' private information, such as a physical or email address, without their explicit permission
* Other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting

## Enforcement Responsibilities

Community leaders are responsible for clarifying and enforcing our standards of acceptable behavior and will take appropriate and fair corrective action in response to any behavior that they deem inappropriate, threatening, offensive, or harmful.

Community leaders have the right and responsibility to remove, edit, or reject comments, commits, code, wiki edits, issues, and other contributions that are not aligned to this Code of Conduct, and will communicate reasons for moderation decisions when appropriate.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all community spaces, and also applies when an individual is officially representing the community in public spaces. Examples of representing our community include using an official e-mail address, posting via an official social media account, or acting as an appointed representative at an online or offline event.

## Enforcement

Instances of abusive, harassing, or otherwise unacceptable behavior may be reported to the community leaders responsible for enforcement at **conduct@auspexai.network**. All complaints will be reviewed and investigated promptly and fairly. See the AuspexAI-specific procedure below for response timelines, the escalation pathway when a Maintainer is the subject of a report, and other operational details.

All community leaders are obligated to respect the privacy and security of the reporter of any incident.

## Enforcement Guidelines

Community leaders will follow these Community Impact Guidelines in determining the consequences for any action they deem in violation of this Code of Conduct:

### 1. Correction

**Community Impact**: Use of inappropriate language or other behavior deemed unprofessional or unwelcome in the community.

**Consequence**: A private, written warning from community leaders, providing clarity around the nature of the violation and an explanation of why the behavior was inappropriate. A public apology may be requested.

### 2. Warning

**Community Impact**: A violation through a single incident or series of actions.

**Consequence**: A warning with consequences for continued behavior. No interaction with the people involved, including unsolicited interaction with those enforcing the Code of Conduct, for a specified period of time. This includes avoiding interactions in community spaces as well as external channels like social media. Violating these terms may lead to a temporary or permanent ban.

### 3. Temporary Ban

**Community Impact**: A serious violation of community standards, including sustained inappropriate behavior.

**Consequence**: A temporary ban from any sort of interaction or public communication with the community for a specified period of time. No public or private interaction with the people involved, including unsolicited interaction with those enforcing the Code of Conduct, is allowed during this period. Violating these terms may lead to a permanent ban.

### 4. Permanent Ban

**Community Impact**: Demonstrating a pattern of violation of community standards, including sustained inappropriate behavior, harassment of an individual, or aggression toward or disparagement of classes of individuals.

**Consequence**: A permanent ban from any sort of public interaction within the community.

## AuspexAI-specific procedure

The body above is the Contributor Covenant 2.1 verbatim. The sections below cover AuspexAI-specific operational detail: scope clarifications, reporting procedure, response timelines, and the escalation pathway when a Maintainer is the subject of a complaint.

### Scope clarifications for AuspexAI community spaces

The Code of Conduct applies in:

- All AuspexAI GitHub repositories (issues, pull requests, discussions, wiki, code review)
- Any official AuspexAI communication channel (mailing list, chat, forum) once established
- Conferences, events, or external venues when an individual is representing AuspexAI
- Communications using an `@auspexai.network` email address or official AuspexAI social media accounts

The Code of Conduct does **not** govern:

- Conduct of Volunteers operating Workers on the AuspexAI network — this is governed by the separate Volunteer Terms of Participation (forthcoming, Phase 2). Network-level abuse (running malicious workers, abusing reputation systems, etc.) is handled through technical enforcement (revocation, ban, trust-tier demotion), not CoC enforcement, though egregious cases may invoke both.
- Conduct outside community spaces and not representing AuspexAI (a contributor's personal social media activity is generally out of scope unless it constitutes harassment of another community member or otherwise affects community spaces)

### Agent behavior in experiments

This Code of Conduct applies to humans participating in AuspexAI community spaces. The conduct of AI agents instantiated by tenant experiments — and the design intent of those agents by the Researchers who author them — is governed by the AuspexAI **Research Ethics Policy** (forthcoming, Phase 0 deliverable, located alongside this document in `auspexai/.github`).

The constraint in summary: Researchers may not design experiments whose primary purpose is to produce behavior that would violate this Code of Conduct were a human to engage in it (harassment, abuse, doxxing, the categories of unacceptable behavior enumerated above). Research that legitimately requires observing such behavior as a *subject of study* — for example, alignment-failure analysis, behavioral-drift detection, jailbreak research, adversarial robustness — is permitted with appropriate containment, output controls, and ethics review. The Research Ethics Policy makes the dual-use distinction explicit and is part of the Approver pool's tenant-acceptance bar.

### Reporting procedure

1. **Where to report**: send an email to `conduct@auspexai.network`. The address is private and read by the AuspexAI Maintainer team only.
2. **What to include**: a description of the incident, when and where it occurred, who was involved, any evidence you can share (links, screenshots, transcripts), and what outcome you are seeking. None of these are required; reports are valid even when minimal.
3. **Confidentiality**: the identity of the reporter is held in confidence by the enforcement chain. Reports are not shared outside enforcement deliberations except with the explicit consent of the reporter or where required by law.
4. **Response timeline**:
   - Acknowledgment of receipt: within 7 calendar days
   - Substantive response (decision, or update on investigation status): within 30 calendar days
   - For ongoing investigations, status updates at least every 30 days until resolution
5. **Outcome**: the reporter is informed of the action taken (consequence selected from the Enforcement Guidelines, or a finding of no violation, or referral elsewhere). Decisions and rationale are documented in the public record where appropriate, with reporter identity redacted unless they explicitly consent to disclosure.

### Escalation pathway when a Maintainer is the subject of a complaint

When a Maintainer is the subject of a CoC complaint, the standard reporting path has a structural conflict — the subject Maintainer cannot fairly be among the people who handle the complaint. AuspexAI commits to the following procedure for these cases.

**During co-Maintainer phase (when at least one Maintainer is not the subject)**:

- The complaint is handled by the Maintainer team excluding the subject Maintainer
- The subject Maintainer is recused entirely from deliberations and decision
- If all Maintainers are subjects of the complaint, the procedure for sole-Maintainer phase below applies

**During sole-Maintainer phase (sole Maintainer is the subject)**:

By adopting this Code of Conduct, the sole Maintainer pre-commits to the following procedure for any complaint where they are the subject:

1. **Acknowledgment and recusal**: the Maintainer publicly acknowledges receipt of the complaint (without disclosing the reporter's identity unless the reporter explicitly consents to disclosure) within 7 days, and recuses from handling it.
2. **Reporter selects the path**: the reporter chooses one of three options. The Maintainer accommodates whichever the reporter prefers.

   **Option A — Reporter-named external advisor**: the reporter names an external advisor of their choosing, who must be a senior member of an unaffiliated OSS, research, or AI-safety community with public credibility on community governance matters. The Maintainer engages with the advisor's investigation in good faith and pre-commits to honoring the advisor's recommendation, even if unfavorable to the Maintainer.

   **Option B — Mutually selected external advisor**: the Maintainer proposes at least three candidate advisors meeting the same profile as Option A. The reporter selects one of the three (or rejects all and falls back to Option A or C). Same engagement and binding-recommendation rules as Option A.

   **Option C — Public escalation**: the reporter chooses to make the complaint public (post it openly, engage broader community attention, contact press if relevant). The Maintainer commits to good-faith engagement with public scrutiny: responding to the complaint publicly, accepting that resolution will be visible to the community, and not retaliating against the reporter for public disclosure.

3. **Binding-by-pre-commitment**: by adopting this Code of Conduct, the Maintainer pre-commits to honoring an advisor's recommendation in cases where Option A or B is invoked, even if the recommendation is unfavorable to the Maintainer. This is a structural constraint on the Maintainer's authority that the project's other governance does not provide during sole-Maintainer phase.
4. **Documentation**: once resolved, the case is documented in the public record with appropriate redactions for reporter privacy. If the reporter chose Option C, the resolution is documented in the same public channel where the complaint was aired.

### Why this procedure exists in this form

AuspexAI is small. We do not have a standing CoC committee, an Ombud, or a Steering body. During the sole-Maintainer phase (Phase 0–1), the only reasonable accountability mechanisms for Maintainer-as-subject complaints are external review or public scrutiny, both selected by the reporter. The pre-commitment to honor advisor recommendations is the structural substitute for an internal accountability body. This will be revisited at Phase 4 entry per `GOVERNANCE.md` §10.

### Anonymous reporting

If a reporter prefers anonymity, the email at `conduct@auspexai.network` accepts reports from any address, and reporter identity is held in confidence as described above. Truly anonymous reports (with no return address) will be handled to the extent possible but cannot be acknowledged or updated.

### What if `conduct@auspexai.network` is unavailable or untrusted

If the reporting address is non-functional, untrusted (e.g., the reporter believes the recipients of conduct@ are themselves involved), or otherwise inappropriate, reporters may instead:

- Open a confidential private security advisory in any AuspexAI repository (GitHub's private vulnerability reporting feature can be used for sensitive non-security communication if necessary)
- Use Option C (public escalation) directly
- Contact a community member they trust who has Maintainer access to relay the report

The integrity of the reporting channel is the Maintainer's responsibility; if reporters consistently feel they cannot use it, that itself is a CoC failure subject to the escalation pathway above.
